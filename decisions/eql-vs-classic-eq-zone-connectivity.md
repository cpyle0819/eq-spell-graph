# This game (EverQuest Legends) has different zone connectivity than classic EverQuest

Don't assume classic-EQ knowledge (Project 1999, Allakhazam, etc.) applies here — verify against eqlwiki.com specifically. Case in point: classic EQ has no Qeynos-side translocator, but EQL added one between East Freeport and South Qeynos in a May 28, 2026 patch (1pp/level fee, not modeled — the planner optimizes for hops, not cost). Zone names, connectivity, and NPC services can all diverge from classic EQ, sometimes recently and without the wiki being fully caught up yet — cross-check multiple pages (individual zone pages *and* patch notes) when something seems off, since new content can lag its own zone-page documentation.

The same "don't trust classic-EQ memory" warning applies to spell tuning, not just zones — e.g. Ward Undead (Paladin L1) showed "35 and 41 damage" in our data, which didn't match live-EQ memory of the spell. Investigated by fetching eqlwiki.com's live page directly: at the time, the wikitext's own two fields *disagreed with each other* — the free-text `description` field said "inflicts 12 damage" while the structured `{{SpellSlotRow}}` effect table said "Decrease Hitpoints by 35 (L5) to 41 (L11)." Our scraper (`scripts/scrape-spell-details.ts`) pulls the `description` field verbatim, and our stored value ("between 35 and 41 damage... 1.37 DPM") matched neither of the wiki's current fields — meaning the wiki's prose had been edited (likely a rebalance) sometime after our last scrape. Not a scraper bug: the scraper faithfully copies whatever the `description` field says at scrape time.

Re-ran `scripts/scrape-spell-details.ts` from scratch (deleted `data/spell-details.json` first — the script resumes from whatever's already in that file, so a normal run only backfills spells missing entirely, it won't refresh stale ones already present) and re-applied migration 011. Ward Undead now correctly reads "12 damage," matching the wiki's current prose. This wasn't an isolated case: **223 of 1,059 spell descriptions changed** in that one re-scrape, mostly the wiki collapsing damage/heal *ranges* down to a single number (e.g. "healing between 47 and 65 hit points" → "healing 65 hit points") and trimming long lore paragraphs to one-liners — reads like a site-wide editorial pass on the description template, not one-off rebalances. Lesson: this data goes stale faster than expected and drifts in bulk, not just spell-by-spell — re-scrape periodically rather than only chasing individual reports, and don't assume a single wrong-looking number is an isolated scrape error.
