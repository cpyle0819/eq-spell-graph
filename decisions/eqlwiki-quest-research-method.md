# How the leveling-guide quests were researched (and what a future scraper needs)

Migration 028's 6 quests (Armor of Ro, Mold of Ro Bracer, Journeyman's Boots, Research Aid, Paladin Epic Quest, Cleric Epic Quest) were hand-authored from real eqlwiki.com pages, not invented — but gathered manually via `WebFetch`, not scraped. This documents the method and its rough edges, for whoever builds an actual scraper later (per CLAUDE.md, no scraping pipeline exists yet — this was one-off manual research).

## Finding which pages to fetch

Started from `public/leveling-guide.html`'s own outbound `eqlwiki.com` links (`grep -n 'eqlwiki\.com/[^"]*[Qq]uest'`) rather than searching the wiki — the leveling guide already curates real quest links with exact page titles, which is a better entry point than guessing titles from an item/quest name. A scraper's first pass could do the same: walk every `eqlwiki.com` link already embedded in this app's own content before trying anything more exploratory.

`WebFetch`, never `WebSearch`, and always with a "quote verbatim" instruction in the prompt — a search-result summary blends in classic-EQ trivia even when domain-restricted, since EQL and classic EQ share most proper nouns. Asking for verbatim quotes at least surfaces exactly what the page said, rather than a paraphrase that could quietly substitute classic-EQ numbers.

## Two very different page shapes

**Item pages are uniform** — one infobox-style stat line per item (`Slot: X / AC: Y / STR: +Z ... WT: ... Size: ... / Class: ... / Race: ...`), a separate flags line (`MAGIC ITEM`, `LORE ITEM`, `NO DROP`/`NO TRADE`), and usually a source/drops-from block. This regularity is exactly what `card-base.js`'s `wikiUrl()` already relies on (title → underscored, percent-encoded URL, same convention `scripts/scrape-spell-details.ts` validated at scale for spells) and what makes item pages the realistic first target for an actual scraper — a small number of regexes over the infobox line would get most of `ItemDetails` (`decisions/item-node-schema.md`) mechanically.

**Quest pages are not uniform.** Same information (giver, zone, level/class gate, turn-in items, rewards, faction) but presented as a prose paragraph in one page (Scarab Armor Quests), a table in another (Armor of Ro's 7-piece armor table), and a numbered step list in a third (both Epic quests). A scraper here needs per-heading extraction with a lot of tolerance, not one fixed template — realistically a first version should assist a human (extract candidate fields, flag ambiguity) rather than write graph nodes unsupervised.

## Gotchas hit doing this by hand

- **A guessed title can 404.** "Runes and Research" (Research Aid's lesser reward) doesn't exist under any title tried. Rather than fabricate that item node, it's left as prose in the quest's `steps` — a scraper needs to verify a page exists (search the wiki, don't just construct a URL from an item name) and needs a defined behavior for "reward mentioned, page not found" (skip the node, same as here, not invent one).
- **A "quest" name isn't always its own authoritative page.** "Mold of Ro Bracer" is an item-page stub whose only quest info is "see Armor of Ro Quests" — the real giver/turn-in/faction data lives on that other page, under a "Mold of Ro Bracer" sub-heading. A scraper needs to follow "Related quest(s)" links rather than trust that an interesting page title is itself the source.
- **One wiki page can cover many sub-quests.** Armor of Ro's page is 7 separate armor-piece quests (different NPC/faction/turn-in each) plus a crafting step, under one URL. There's no purely mechanical rule for "one graph quest per wiki page" vs. "one per sub-heading" — here, a sub-quest only became its own graph node when something *outside* the wiki (the leveling guide itself) called it out individually (Mold of Ro Bracer); the rest of the set was compressed into the page-level "Armor of Ro" quest. A scraper can't make that call alone; it needs either a human decision per page or a default (probably: one node per page, sub-headings folded into `steps` text) that a human overrides for cases like this one.
- **`WebFetch` summarizes through a small model, not raw markup.** Fine for prose, risky for exact numbers (a summarizer can transpose or drop a stat). A real scraper should hit the MediaWiki API directly (`action=raw` or `action=parse`) for lossless wikitext instead of a rendered-and-summarized page.
- **Class/race restrictions aren't consistently formatted.** Some pages spell them out in a resolved Categories tag (Small Scarab Helm: "available to Bard, Cleric, ..."); others give space-separated abbreviations in the infobox line (`WAR PAL RNG SHD BRD ROG`). A scraper needs an abbreviation → full-name table (`WAR`→`warrior`, `SHD`→`shadow knight`, etc.) — this repo doesn't have one centralized yet; migration 028 resolved these by hand per item.
- **"Minimum level" can be technically-true-but-useless.** Journeyman's Boots states "Minimum Level: 1" but is realistically unreachable before ~30 — the quest node uses the practical number (30, itself a direct quote: "realistically level 30+") since the tool's filter exists to help someone find quests near their actual level, not recite an unenforced game rule. A scraper should preserve both the technical and practical numbers if both are stated and let a human pick, rather than silently choosing one.
- **Multi-tier progression pages (checked directly, don't re-attempt expecting different results):** questlines with several sequential sub-quests on one page — Coldain Ring/Prayer Shawl Quests, Monk Sash/Headband/Shackle Quests, Shaman Skull Quests, Necromancer Skullcap Quests, Warrior Pike Quests, Crusader's Tests — only ever state one blanket range for the *whole* series ("30+ to start", "1-25 (progresses)"), never a level per individual tier, even under a targeted per-heading/per-table-row search. The rare exception is an incidental aside buried in walkthrough prose (Necromancer Skullcap Rank 5: "at least level 20 to finish this part") — one data point in a 5-9 tier chain, not enough to back-derive the rest. Scaled Mystic Armor Quests is the one page with real escalating per-piece data, but it's mob kill-level ranges for farmed reagents, not a stated quest level — using it would mean inferring a number the wiki never actually asserts, a different (and riskier) move than picking between two numbers it does state.
