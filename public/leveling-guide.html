The Road to Fifty

Notes for a first character, kept for anyone walking the same road since.

The EverQuest experience has always been full of deep dungeon delves, epic exploration, and great gear. But it's not a complete adventure unless you dive into the in-depth tradeskill system, trade languages with other Norrathians, spend hours fishing off a pier, and make great friends along the way. The journey to fifty and beyond is full of excitement and surprises, and the leveling guide and tips below are just the beginning.

The Trail

Every class you own is leveling together while you find your footing. Start anywhere near your home city. Partying up? A Magician and Cleric pair is fast and forgiving: the pet tanks, the Cleric keeps it upright. Trios built around a Paladin or Shadow Knight with a Cleric or Shaman alongside come up again and again in beta chatter too, self-sustaining enough to solo through the rough patches once you've got all three classes in your loadout.

The Estate of Unrest becomes the obvious anchor: once a dwarven noble's home, now one of the great undead havens on Norrath, with a strong experience bonus and camps that scale right alongside you. Solusek's Eye, home to fire goblins and an intrepid gnome mining company chasing the mineral wealth underneath them, and Mistmoore Castle, vampire lord Mayong Mistmoore's mazelike stronghold, carry the rest of the climb through your low twenties. High Keep, the crumbling seat of an empire that once ruled central Antonica before centuries of orc and gnoll raids reduced it to Highpass Hold, is worth a look too for a change of scenery.

Ocean of Tears' Elite Goblin Island picks up the pace, especially with an Enchanter along to charm the reptiles; the ocean itself is the old boat route carrying travelers between Antonica and Faydwer. Lower Guk, the tunnel-riddled Ruins of Ancient Guk that connect down to Innothule Swamp, is worth lingering at from 40–44: exactly the kind of single-spot camp multiclassing lets a trio hold down alone.

The Hole, officially the Ruins of Old Paineel (a hole the Heretics punched straight into Odus during their war with Erudin), or the Efreeti camp in Nagafen's Lair, Sol B to anyone who's been around, deep in the Lavastorm Mountains where the Fire Giants serve Lord Nagafen himself, close out the climb before the Planes open their doors at 46.
Off the beaten path: Najena, carved out by the dark elf magician of the same name as a den for her and her allies' experiments; Permafrost, a castle being slowly swallowed by glacial ice with goblins now denning in its ruins; Kedge Keep, the drowned fortress of an aquatic race that died out centuries ago; and Stonebrunt Mountains, spiritual home of the Kejekan people atop Mount Klaw, all pull solid experience if you'd rather skip the well-known stops. Traveling solo? The game's built for it. The easiest difficulty lets you clear content made for a group at your own pace, and a private instance is always one option away.

Notable Finds Along the Way

Soft Wicker armor The Warrens, a kobold warren Clan Kolbok dug into the heart of Odus, has its own gear set: Soft Wicker armor, leather-equivalent with a look you won't see anywhere else, weapons included. Armor of Ro Rathe Mountains hides more than danger along its southern route between Freeport and Qeynos. Paladins camp there working toward a Mold of Ro from the Temple of Solusek Ro, combined with a crafted Bar of Ronium at a forge. Taskmaster's whip & earring Crushbone, the orc clan camp that's been a thorn in Faydwer's side for as long as anyone remembers. Its Taskmaster drops a 5/30 whip and his own brass earring. Mold of Ro Bracer Lake Rathetear, a small inland sea in the wilds of Antonica: its goblin net masters drop Deepwater Goblin Nets. Turn two in to David, keeper of the Bracers of Ro at the Rathe Mountains paladin camp. Research Aid Northern Karana sits at the crossroads of half the continent's bridge traffic, and its resident scholar Mrysila runs a repeatable turn-in: lightstones (a willowisp drop) buy spell research tomes, greater lightstones buy the Concordance of Research.

Beyond the Kill Count

None of this is about experience per hour. It's what's easy to miss if a leveling guide is the only lens you ever look through.

Tradeskills Eight trades to dabble in (Alchemy, Baking, Blacksmithing, Brewing, Fletching, Jewelcrafting, Pottery, Tailoring), and the game already nudges you toward one: Blacksmithing leans on Strength, Fletching on Dexterity. Push past 200 in one and the rest lock there until you buy the Crafting Mastery AA, so most crafters commit to a single trade instead of spreading thin. Fishing A rod, bait, and any water will do. The smallest fishable puddle in the game sits, of all places, in the tunnel between West Freeport and North Freeport's newbie areas. Ocean of Tears turns up real saltwater catches, and North Qeynos is home to the game's infamous koalindl, one of a handful of "fish" you wade out and kill rather than reel in. Languages Every race speaks its own tongue on top of Common, and the honest way to learn one is still the old way: sit in a group with a fluent friend and have them speak it at you (the /gs command) until it stops reading as "an unknown tongue." That happens around skill 25. Ak'Anon Worth the walk even empty-handed. A gnomish city built into the mountainside, half fortress and half laboratory, its clockwork machines still being tinkered with after generations of work. Temple of Solusek Ro Not every zone is about killing something. This one isn't built for hunting or vendors at all, just for talking to the people who live there. A strange, quiet stop if you've only ever passed through Norrath's dungeons. Class Epic Quests Every class has one waiting eventually: long, group-driven questlines through the game's hardest dungeons for a genuinely unique weapon. Paladins chase the Fiery Defender, Clerics the Water Sprinkler of Nem Ankh. Worth building toward from level one, even if the actual grind is a long way off. Race unlocks Want to play something you didn't start as? Every race has its own list of factions to max out, and EQL is generous about how you get there: charm a faction's enemies, sneak past its guards, or swap your loadout for a class that faction likes better. A slow side project worth chipping away at while you level. Southern Felwithe The high elf capital rises out of Greater Faydark's permanent fog with walls that glow faintly gold on their own. Even players who never touch an elf tend to wander through once, just to see it. Neriak The dark elf answer to Felwithe: an entire city carved out of mountain rock that has never once seen natural sunlight, and the dark elves like it that way.
