The Revamped Road

EverQuest Legends rebuilt five classic zones from the ground up in mid-2026. Here's what changed, in level order.

Blackburrow, Crushbone, Najena, Splitpaw Lair, and Plane of Sky each got a real overhaul this year, not a cosmetic pass: new named enemies, denser spawns, and loot tables pushed well past what those zones used to give up. A new character can walk all five in order and never outlevel what they offer. The trail below covers each one and what specifically changed, including the new gear worth planning a stop for.

A Few Tricks

Quick Buff
if any of your classes cast buffs, grab this AA early. One button applies every beneficial spell on your bar to the whole group at once, outside of combat.
Spell sets
save different spell loadouts from the spellbook window and swap between them anytime with /memspellset. Hotbars are just as flexible: open extra ones and set each up independently instead of rebuilding your whole bar for a different situation.
Autoskill
abilities like Backstab or Smite don't have to be clicked by hand. /autoskill <ability> on ties them to auto-attack, firing automatically the moment they're off cooldown.

The Trail

Blackburrow, the gnoll stronghold between Everfrost and Qeynos Hills, was rebalanced in June 2026: population density was retuned for a smoother climb across the whole 4–20 range, and its old loot table was upgraded rather than left as a classic holdover. Crushbone's July 2026 update raised spawn and respawn rates throughout the orc encampment and extended its range to 5–22. Emperor Crush now drops a , and the zone's Orc Trainer carries a (+10 AC, +10 magic resist).

Najena's June 2026 touch-up smoothed the pacing of its key-and-lock progression from 8 to 35, adding new named enemies on top of the existing cast. Najena herself now drops a and a .

Splitpaw Lair's July 2026 overhaul spans five difficulty bands across its 20–42 range, with new rare spawns and better equipment throughout the gnoll hierarchy. The Ishva Mal's (+50 mana) and Verishe Mal Executioner's (2H Slashing, 26 dmg) are among the new loot, alongside a (+10 INT, +5 WIS) deeper in the gnoll hierarchy.

Plane of Sky's June 2026 rework changed how its eight-island key system behaves: a key now stays on your keyring permanently once used, and drops from each island's boss instead of trash mobs. The class epic-quest requirements at each island were simplified to a handful of wind runes plus one or two specific items, and most of the zone's best loot now comes from finishing those quests rather than direct boss drops.

Beyond the Kill Count

None of this is about experience per hour. It's what's easy to miss if a leveling guide is the only lens you ever look through.

Tradeskills Eight trades to dabble in (Alchemy, Baking, Blacksmithing, Brewing, Fletching, Jewelcrafting, Pottery, Tailoring), and the game already nudges you toward one: Blacksmithing leans on Strength, Fletching on Dexterity. Push past 200 in one and the rest lock there until you buy the Crafting Mastery AA, so most crafters commit to a single trade instead of spreading thin. Fishing A rod, bait, and any water will do. The smallest fishable puddle in the game sits, of all places, in the tunnel between West Freeport and North Freeport's newbie areas. Ocean of Tears turns up real saltwater catches, and North Qeynos is home to the game's infamous koalindl, one of a handful of "fish" you wade out and kill rather than reel in. Languages Every race speaks its own tongue on top of Common, and the honest way to learn one is still the old way: sit in a group with a fluent friend and have them speak it at you (the /gs command) until it stops reading as "an unknown tongue." That happens around skill 25. Ak'Anon Worth the walk even empty-handed. A gnomish city built into the mountainside, half fortress and half laboratory, its clockwork machines still being tinkered with after generations of work. Temple of Solusek Ro Not every zone is about killing something. This one isn't built for hunting or vendors at all, just for talking to the people who live there. A strange, quiet stop if you've only ever passed through Norrath's dungeons. Class Epic Quests Every class has one waiting eventually: long, group-driven questlines through the game's hardest dungeons for a genuinely unique weapon. Paladins chase the Fiery Defender, Clerics the Water Sprinkler of Nem Ankh. Worth building toward from level one, even if the actual grind is a long way off. Race unlocks Want to play something you didn't start as? Every race has its own list of factions to max out, and EQL is generous about how you get there: charm a faction's enemies, sneak past its guards, or swap your loadout for a class that faction likes better. A slow side project worth chipping away at while you level. Fippy Darkpaw Every North Qeynos newcomer eventually runs into Fippy Darkpaw, or his stand-in Djerr Darkpaw, camped right outside the gates looking for a fight. A classic first kill, still. Giants in the Commonlands West Commonlands hides hill giants in its southern reaches, big and somehow still easy to walk right past without noticing. A rite of passage the first time one notices you first. Kithicor Forest, after dark From 8 PM to 6 AM game-time, Kithicor Forest empties of its usual orcs and fills instead with level 35+ undead, a holdover from the Plane of Hate's opening known as the Haunting of Kithicor. Not somewhere to wander into casually after dark, but worth seeing once.
